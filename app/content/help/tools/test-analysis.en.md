## When do you use this tool?

Use this tool **after a test**, when you want to know which questions worked well and which you should look at again. The tool explains the numbers of the test analysis: the p-value, the Rit and Rir per question, and Cronbach's alpha. The **AI computes nothing itself**: the numbers come from the utility "Analyse test results per question" or from your test system.

## How does it work?

1. **Open the utility** "Analyse test results per question" and paste the scores: one row per candidate, one column per question. The utility removes names and student numbers; the score list stays in your browser.
2. **Click "Explain this".** This tool opens with the numbers per question filled in.
3. **Add, if you like,** what kind of test it was (for multiple choice: how many options), and paste the questions that stand out, with the key.
4. **Read the explanation:** which questions to look at first, why, and what you can do.

## What the tool does and doesn't do

- **Does:** explain what the numbers say together, with thresholds as rules of thumb. With a negative Rit or Rir it points to the answer key first, and with few candidates it warns that the numbers vary.
- **Doesn't:** compute numbers, drop a question or change the results. You decide that, with the examination board where needed.

## Tips

- **If a question goes out or you score it differently,** recompute the numbers with the utility. The pass mark may change too.
- **Low alpha?** Look at the number of questions as well. A short test, or a test on very different learning outcomes, has a lower alpha by nature.
