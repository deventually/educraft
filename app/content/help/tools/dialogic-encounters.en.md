## When to use this tool

Dialogic Encounters lets you **learn a theory by interviewing the theorist themselves**. Use it when you don't just want to *know* what Piaget or Vygotsky thought, but to experience how their assumptions play out in teaching decisions.

## How it works

It's a **chat tool**. Pick a theorist; the AI plays that person and engages you in dialogue. Start with a suggestion or type your own question. You can stop the conversation or regenerate a reply.

## The theorists

Pick the theorist whose perspective fits your question. A short characterisation per school of thought.

**Constructivism — knowledge is actively built**

- **Jean Piaget** — learning moves through developmental stages; the learner constructs knowledge via assimilation and accommodation.
- **Jerome Bruner** — discovery learning, the spiral curriculum and scaffolding; knowledge in enactive, iconic and symbolic forms.
- **Lev Vygotsky** — learning is social; the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) and the role of language and guidance.
- **John Dewey** — learning by doing; experience, reflection and inquiry connected to the real world.

**Constructionism — learning by making**

- **Seymour Papert** — knowledge grows most strongly by building and sharing something; creator of Logo and computational thinking.

**Social learning**

- **Albert Bandura** — learning by observation and modelling; the importance of self-efficacy (belief in one's own ability).

**Behaviorism — behaviour shaped by consequences**

- **B.F. Skinner** — operant conditioning: reinforcement and punishment shape behaviour; programmed instruction.
- **Ivan Pavlov** — classical conditioning: learning through association between stimuli.

**Cognitivism — processing information in memory**

- **Robert Gagné** — the Conditions of Learning and Nine Events of Instruction; learning hierarchies.
- **David Ausubel** — meaningful learning rather than rote; advance organizers link new material to prior knowledge.
- **John Sweller** — Cognitive Load Theory: working memory is limited; manage the load in your instruction.
- **Jeroen van Merriënboer** — the 4C/ID model for complex learning; integrate knowledge and skills in whole tasks.
- **Paul A. Kirschner** — evidence-informed teaching; advocate of direct instruction and critic of minimal guidance and education myths.

**Experiential learning**

- **David Kolb** — the learning cycle: experience, reflect, conceptualise, experiment.

**Humanism — the learner at the centre**

- **Carl Rogers** — learner-centred and facilitative; meaningful learning in a safe, accepting climate.

**Andragogy — adults learn differently**

- **Malcolm Knowles** — adults learn in a self-directed way, from experience and around relevant, practical problems.

## Which encounter fits your question?

- **How do learners build knowledge themselves?** → Piaget or Bruner
- **How do I design collaborative learning and scaffolding?** → Vygotsky or Bruner
- **Project work, making and programming** → Papert or Dewey
- **Motivation through role models and confidence** → Bandura
- **Behaviour, habits, practice and reinforcement** → Skinner or Pavlov
- **Structuring a lesson step by step** → Gagné
- **Designing complex skills and whole tasks** → Van Merriënboer
- **Preventing working-memory overload** → Sweller
- **Connecting new material to prior knowledge** → Ausubel
- **What does the evidence say? Does discovery learning work?** → Kirschner
- **Reflecting on placements and practical experience** → Kolb or Dewey
- **Autonomy, safety and the learner at the centre** → Rogers
- **Teaching adults and professionals** → Knowles

## Tips

- **Ask follow-ups** ("how would you handle this in class?") — the depth is in probing, not in a single question.
- **Challenge the theorist**: ask about weak spots or critiques of the theory.
- **Compare perspectives**: run a second conversation with a theorist from another school of thought and note the differences.
- It's a role-play — use it to practise thinking, not as a source for quotations.
