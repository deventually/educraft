## When do you use this tool?

An AI can write a report, but not a defense of your own choices. With this tool a **critical but friendly expert** from the field has a student defend their own work out loud: a report, internship, project, professional product or lab. Why this approach, what does your data say, which errors could be in it, what can and can't you conclude.

Students don't work in LimeOnIt. You use the tool yourself: to practise, to show how it works, and to make the text for your students.

## How does it work?

1. **Choose the kind of work** and fill in the subject or programme and the learning objective. If you have a course, you can take over its learning outcomes.
2. **Optionally name the choices** the student must be able to defend, and choose how long the conversation lasts. With **Student first pastes the key figures** the expert asks after the opening for a few figures or findings, without names, and probes more sharply on them. Never the report itself.
3. **Play the student yourself** to see how the expert probes.
4. **Copy for your students:** the button under *Your settings* copies the whole text, with your input in it. Your students paste it into their own AI chat and hold the defense there.

## What the tool does and doesn't do

- **Does:** one question at a time, and probing vague answers such as "human error" or "it was right".
- **Does:** not approve a wrong answer, but ask a question that lets the student find the error. If the student is stuck, the expert helps in three steps.
- **Doesn't:** write the defense for the student, or skip the defense when the student asks.
- **Doesn't:** give a grade or "passed". The closing says whether the work is finished, or which concept the student should review. You assess.

## Tips

- **Ask for the conversation:** have students hand in the conversation next to their report, as the book suggests.
- **No personal data:** the expert has not read the work and does not ask for it. A student doesn't need to name anyone.
