## When do you use this tool?

Use this tool when your group differs in prior knowledge or pace, and you want to give **one task or explanation** in three variants: with extra support, the base and with extra challenge. All three assess **the same learning goal**. You get a **draft** with the student texts written out, and a tip on using the variants in class. You know the group and decide who does which variant.

## How does it work?

1. **Fill in the learning goal**, with an observable verb (calculates, explains, assesses). With "understands" or "knows" the tool proposes a sharper wording and builds the variants on it.
2. **Paste your task or explanation** as you give it to students now. It becomes the base variant.
3. **Say what you know about differences in the group**, if you like. Describe the group, not people: no names.

## What the tool does and does not do

- **Does:** support that leaves the thinking to the student (a worked example, a step plan, sentence starters), challenge that is more complex instead of more work, and neutral names (Route A, B, C) instead of "easy" and "hard" (Tomlinson, 2017).
- **Does:** say honestly when your task does not assess the learning goal, or when a wish would lower the goal.
- **Does not:** replace your learning goal, invent sources or add material your goal does not ask for.

## Tips

- **Let students switch.** A short entry question helps to choose, but a student may move to another route. Next time the groups are different again.
- **Check the subject content and the answers** before you use the variants. You are the subject expert.

> Original LimeOnIt instrument. Grounding: Tomlinson, C. A. (2017), *How to Differentiate Instruction in Academically Diverse Classrooms* (3rd edition), ASCD.
