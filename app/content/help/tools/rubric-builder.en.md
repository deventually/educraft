## When to use this tool

Use this tool when you need an **assessment rubric** for a learning outcome: for a new assignment, or to improve an existing rubric. You get a **draft**: criteria, levels with observable descriptions, points, a pass mark and pass rules, plus a short self-check. You decide on the rubric, possibly together with your examination board.

## How it works

1. **Fill in the learning outcome**, with an observable verb (analyses, designs, argues). If it says "understands" or "knows", the tool first proposes a sharper wording and doesn't build a rubric yet.
2. **Describe the assignment**: what does the student make or do?
3. **Choose the number of levels** (2, 3, 4 or 5) and optionally fill in your points, pass mark and knock-out criteria. If you leave that empty, the tool makes a proposal, and says so.
4. **Already have a rubric?** Paste it in the last field. The tool first looks for overlapping or vague levels, criteria that don't belong to the outcome and points that don't add up, then makes an improved version.

## What the tool does and doesn't do

- **Does:** link the criteria to your learning outcome, guard the cognitive level of the verb (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001), and describe levels you can see in the work (Brookhart, 2013).
- **Doesn't:** replace your learning outcome, invent standards or norms, or give a grade.

## Tips

- **The table has the same shape as an export from the rubric editor.** So you can put it next to a saved rubric or paste it into an assessment tool.
- **Try the draft on two or three real pieces of work** before you adopt it: does each piece fit exactly one level?

> Original LimeOnIt instrument. Grounding: Brookhart, S. M. (2013), *How to Create and Use Rubrics for Formative Assessment and Grading* (ASCD); Anderson, L. W. & Krathwohl, D. R. (2001), *A Taxonomy for Learning, Teaching, and Assessing* (Longman).
