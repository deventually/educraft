## When to use this tool

Use this engagement check when you want to know **how much active thinking** an assignment, lesson or activity asks of your students. For example before you hand out an assignment again, or when you suspect students mostly copy or reread.

## How it works

Upload your assignment or lesson plan as Word or PDF, or paste the text. Optionally fill in the learning objective. For six learning principles you get a level from *Absent* (0) to *High* (3), each with a reason from your text:

1. retrieval practice;
2. spaced practice;
3. interleaving;
4. dual coding;
5. concrete examples;
6. elaboration.

LimeOnIt computes the **score out of 18** itself, not the AI. Below it are two or three improvements for exactly this assignment.

## Tips

- **Only what is in the text counts.** If you do something in class that isn't in the assignment, add it briefly.
- **A low score is not a verdict.** Not every assignment has to score high on all six principles. Pick one or two improvements that fit.
- **Want to rebuild the assignment?** Carry on with [Design an AI learning activity](/help/cognitive-architect) or [Differentiate a task](/help/differentiation) as the next step.
- **Choose your course**, and the learning objective is filled in and the result is linked to the course.

> Source: the *Cognitive Engagement Rubric* in Appendix G of the chapter *From Oracle to Socratic Partner* by Andy Van Schaack & Roman Sarlo in *The Pedagogical Promptbook*.
