## When to use this tool

Use this tool when you have an **assignment brief or student guide** that students find hard to follow: long sentences, hidden requirements, submission info in three places. You get a **draft** in plain language (reading level B1 by default), with what the student is going to do, why, the steps, the requirements and how to submit.

## How it works

1. **Upload or paste the whole assignment**, including the rubric and the submission info.
2. **Choose the reading level.** B1 is simple, plain language. Choose B2 or C1 when the subject language itself is part of the learning.
3. **Name what must stay word for word**, such as terms students must learn. They stay, with a short explanation at A2 and B1.

## What the tool does and doesn't do

- **Does:** the same requirements in other words. A requirement check shows, per requirement in your text, where it is in the new version.
- **Does:** say what is missing ("You name no deadline") and which requirements contradict each other. That place then says *[to be filled in by the teacher: …]*.
- **Doesn't:** invent a deadline, length or criterion, leave out a requirement or make it stricter, or choose between two requirements that clash.

## Tips

- **Go through the requirement check** before you share the assignment. You know what you meant.
- **A vague criterion** ("of good quality") stays as it is. Under the open points you find a suggestion to make it observable; take it over yourself if you want.
