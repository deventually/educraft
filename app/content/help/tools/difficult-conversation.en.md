## When to use this tool

Use this tool when you want to practise a **difficult conversation** before the real one: about study progress and study advice, bad news about an assessment, a suspicion of fraud, or a student who is stuck. The AI plays a **made-up student**. You lead the conversation.

## How it works

1. **Choose the kind of conversation** and describe the situation, **with no name or student number**.
2. **Choose how the student reacts:** calm, emotional or defensive. From there the student responds to what you do: if you listen, the student tells you more.
3. **Optionally fill in what you want to practise**, for example "giving the bad news quickly and clearly".
4. **Open the conversation** as you would in real life. When you are done, type **"Give feedback"**. The AI then steps out of the role.

## What the tool does and does not do

- **Does:** feedback with **verbatim quotes** of what you said: what went well, what could be better (with a sentence you could say instead) and one thing to practise.
- **Does:** for bad news, the six steps of SPIKES as a guide. SPIKES comes from medicine, for doctors who deliver bad news.
- **Does not:** give legal or medical advice, or say what the outcome of a fraud procedure or study advice will be. Your programme's rules decide that.

## Tips

- **Try again:** after the feedback, ask to start over and practise that one point.
- **It is still an exercise.** A real student reacts differently. Use the feedback as a mirror, not as a judgement.

> Original LimeOnIt instrument. Basis for bad news: Baile, W. F., Buckman, R., Lenzi, R., Glober, G., Beale, E. A., & Kudelka, A. P. (2000), *SPIKES—A Six-Step Protocol for Delivering Bad News*, The Oncologist 5(4), 302–311.
