## When do you use this tool?

Use this tool when you build a **written test** for one or more learning outcomes. The tool works in two steps. First it builds a **test blueprint**: for each learning outcome the cognitive level, the question type, the number of questions and the points. Then it writes the **questions** that follow the blueprint, with an answer model. You get a **draft**; you decide on the test.

## How does it work?

1. **Enter your learning outcomes**, one per line, with an observable verb (calculates, explains, chooses). The tool leaves out an outcome with "understands" or "knows" and proposes a sharper wording.
2. **Paste the material** or describe the concepts, formulas and examples. The questions come only from what is there.
3. **Choose the number of questions and the question types**, and optionally fill in points, test duration and weighting. If you leave that empty, the tool makes a proposal, and says so.
4. **Read the blueprint** in step 1. The questions follow in step 2.

## What the tool does and doesn't do

- **Does:** test every learning outcome at the cognitive level of its verb (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001), and write multiple-choice questions with exactly one correct answer, no clues and plausible distractors. Every distractor comes with why it is wrong (Haladyna, Downing & Rodriguez, 2002).
- **Doesn't:** replace learning outcomes, make up questions beyond your material, or write multiple-choice questions for an outcome that asks students to make something. Multiple choice does not measure that, and the tool says so.

## Tips

- **Have a colleague who knows the subject review the questions** before students take them: does every question really have only one correct answer?
- **Look at the results per question after the test.** A question nearly everyone gets right or wrong, or that strong students get wrong more often, deserves a second look.

(2002), A review of multiple-choice item-writing guidelines for classroom assessment, *Applied Measurement in Education*, 15(3), 309–334.
