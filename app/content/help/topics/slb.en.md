# Study career coaching

As a study career coach, LimeOnIt helps you mostly with the group: preparing sessions, preparing and practising conversations, and knowing where to refer a student. LimeOnIt keeps **no track per student** and stores no conversation reports: study career conversations are often about health, home and money, and study progress is already in your institution's student information system.

## Study career coaching as a course

If you teach study career coaching in group sessions, set it up as an ordinary course:

1. Under **Courses**, click **New course** and give it a name, for example "Study career coaching year 1".
2. Choose **Design in broad outline** and describe in a few sentences what the sessions are about. Name the three strands study career coaching usually has: **self-direction** (planning, reflecting, asking for help), **professional identity** (what the profession asks and whether it suits the student) and **study skills** (studying, working together, using feedback). The AI proposes learning outcomes; you choose what to keep and edit the text.
3. On the Overview tab, put your sessions in the timetable and let **Next step: the lesson series** propose a series of sessions.
4. Work out each session with *Prepare a lesson*.

If your programme assesses study career coaching with a portfolio or reflection, add it to the course's tests. See also [Courses and the alignment spine](/help/courses).

## Preparing and practising conversations

- **Preparing:** *Prepare a conversation* gives a set of questions for a starting conversation, a progress conversation, a threatening negative study advice, doubts about quitting or choosing a direction or internship. You choose the situation, never the student: do not fill in names or personal details.
- **Practising:** with *Practise a difficult conversation* you rehearse the conversation with an AI student, for example bringing a negative study advice, a student who is stuck or talking about dropping out.

LimeOnIt helps you hold the conversation, not make the decision on a study advice. The programme makes that decision.

## Referring on: the referral guide

Some questions are not for the study career coach but for a counter of your institution: study delay, mental health, a disability, unwanted behaviour or money. Which counter that is, your administrator fills in under **Admin → Organisation**. You see the counters below, and also under the result of *Prepare a conversation*. The AI never names a counter itself: referring is up to you.
