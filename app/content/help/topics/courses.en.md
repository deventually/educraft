# Courses and the alignment spine

A **course** records three things and shows whether they line up:

- **Learning outcomes:** what the student can do at the end, with the Bloom level you expect.
- **Teaching activities:** the lessons and assignments, with the week and the learning outcomes they teach.
- **Assessments:** formative or summative, with the learning outcomes they test and at what level. Also say whether an assessment is **supervised**, such as an exam in a hall or an oral exam. Your review team then sees how easily a student could leave the work to AI.

## The course page

The course page has five tabs, each for one task:

- **Overview:** the progress, the course details, the schedule, importing from an existing document and your lessons and tests per week.
- **Learning outcomes:** what the student can do at the end, with the success criteria.
- **Lessons and tests:** your lessons and assignments, and your tests with their blueprint, parts and documents.
- **Check:** the coverage matrix, the findings and your review team. A number next to it is the number of findings.
- **Documents and sharing:** the module guide and module handbook, evidence for your dossier, work you made, the programme, sharing with your team and who works along.

## Progress

At the top of the Overview tab is the **Progress**: how many steps of your course are done. The steps follow *backwards design*, so you start from the goal and work back to the lessons:

1. **Course details:** the study load in EC.
2. **Learning outcomes:** what the student can do at the end.
3. **Tests:** a summative test that assesses every learning outcome.
4. **Lessons and assignments:** every learning outcome comes back in a lesson.
5. **Week planning:** every lesson and test is in a week.
6. **Check:** no points of attention left.

Under **Next step** you see what is best to do now, how to do it and a button to where you do it. Want to look further yourself? Click a step or go to a tab: you do not have to follow the order. Your list of courses shows for each course how many steps are done and what the next step is.

## Schedule

Under **Schedule** on the Overview tab you record once how the course runs: the number of weeks, the sessions per week, the length of a session, the group size and the delivery mode. Everything is optional, because every programme works differently. When you open **Prepare a lesson** from a lesson, the lesson length, group size and delivery mode are filled in, as are that lesson's learning outcomes, its title and the lessons of the weeks before it. You can still change everything.

## The checks

After every change LimeOnIt checks whether everything lines up. You get a point of attention when:

- a learning outcome does not appear in any lesson or assignment;
- a learning outcome is not tested in any **summative** assessment;
- a summative assessment tests a learning outcome in an earlier week than the first lesson about it;
- an assessment tests a learning outcome below that outcome's Bloom level;
- a lesson or assignment serves no learning outcome;
- the weights of the summative assessments do not add up to 100%;
- the planned hours exceed the study load (one EC is 28 hours of study).

The checks are **suggestions**. LimeOnIt changes nothing in your course; you decide. No AI is involved: the checks only work with what you fill in yourself.

## Bloom

The levels follow the revised Bloom taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001): remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate and create. A level is optional. Without one, LimeOnIt skips the level check for that learning outcome.

## The coverage matrix

On the course page is a table with the learning outcomes in the rows and the activities and assessments in the columns. A ✓ means the activity or assessment covers that learning outcome, with the level if you filled it in.

## Starting from an existing module

Redesigning a module? Start from what exists. On the course page, choose **Import from existing documents** and paste the text or choose the files: a course catalogue (OER), study guide, module guide, module handbook, test blueprint or marking model. You can choose several at once, for example a test blueprint and marking model at once. The files are read on your computer; only the text goes to the AI.

The AI prepares a proposal: the module code and study year, learning outcomes with their success criteria, assessments with their form, pass mark, conditions and resit, and the parts with their criteria for a pass and a fail. Whatever is not verbatim in the document is marked. You tick what goes into the course.

If a learning outcome or assessment is already in your course, it is not added twice. With **Fill in empty fields** you only complete what is still empty, such as the parts from a marking model for an assessment you took from the study guide earlier. What you had filled in stays as it is.

## Module guide and module handbook

Under **Module guide and module handbook** on the Documents and sharing tab you fill in the details of the module: module code, study year, study phase, module leader, examiner(s), a description of the content and the literature (one per line). An empty line in the description starts a new paragraph. With **Export module guide** you first see a preview and then choose Word, PDF (through the print window) or Markdown.

The module guide puts it all together: the module details with the credits, every learning outcome with its success criteria, the description, and under **Assessment** a table with every test (form, kind, week, weight, pass mark), the examiners and per test the conditions and the resit. No AI is involved, and whatever you leave empty is left out.

The **module handbook** (*Export module handbook*) is the longer version for the student: the module details, an introduction (the description), study load and length, the learning outcomes with success criteria, the course week by week (every activity and test with its learning outcomes), per test its form, week, weight, grading, pass mark, resit, how it is taken, conditions and parts, and the literature. Running text such as themes or explanations of assignments you then add yourself in Word.

## Test blueprint and marking model

Under each test you make a **test blueprint** with one click and, once the test has parts, a **marking model**. You first see a preview, then choose Word, PDF (through the print window) or Markdown. No AI is involved: the documents come straight from your course, so they always match what you entered. What you leave empty is left out.

- **Success criteria** go with a learning outcome, one per line. If you use codes, start the line with the code and a colon, for example `S-An 1.1: The student …`.
- **Details for the blueprint and the marking model** sit under the test: the form of the test, the test owner, the assessment (grade or pass/fail), the pass mark, the conditions for a pass, how the test is taken and the resit. The module code and the study year go under **Module guide and module handbook**.
- **Parts** are what the student makes or does, with the learning outcomes it shows, a weight, whether it is a knock-out, and when it is a pass or a fail.

The blueprint has a row per learning outcome with the success criteria, the level, the weight, the assessment criteria and the parts. The level is the mastery level from your subject pack if you filled it in, otherwise the Bloom level. A part with more than one learning outcome counts towards each of them. If the test has questions, the weight comes from the points per question, and the specification per question follows, with the points per learning outcome and cognitive level.

The marking model has a table per part with what a fail and what a pass is, and puts the conditions and knock-outs at the top.

## Linking work

On a tool page with a learning-outcome field, pick your course under **Course**. LimeOnIt then fills in the course's learning outcomes, one per line; remove what doesn't fit. What you generate is linked to the course. You can also link or unlink work afterwards on **Earlier work**, to the whole course or to one activity or assessment. The course page lists it all under **Linked work**.

## Get a review

Under **Review team** on the course page, critical colleagues look at your course, for example an **Assessment expert** (assessment blueprint, Bloom spread, wording, weighting) and a **Designer** (constructive alignment, active learning, study load). They are AI and run on the model you pick. You choose who is on your review team; what each reviewer does is in [Your review team](/help/review-team).

- Each piece of advice points at a learning outcome, activity or assessment in your own course, or at the course as a whole.
- For each piece of advice you choose **Agree** or **Disagree**, with a reason if you like. Agreeing only records your decision; nothing in your course changes.
- Advice you disagreed with does not come back in a later review of the same course.
- If you change the course after a review, you see a notice. Then get a new review.

The fixed checks above stay facts; the review is an opinion you weigh against your own judgement.
