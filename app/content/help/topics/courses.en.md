# Courses and the alignment spine

A **course** records three things and shows whether they line up:

- **Learning outcomes:** what the student can do at the end, with the Bloom level you expect.
- **Teaching activities:** the lessons and assignments, with the week and the learning outcomes they teach.
- **Assessments:** formative or summative, with the learning outcomes they test and at what level.

## The checks

After every change LimeOnIt checks whether everything lines up. You get a point of attention when:

- a learning outcome does not appear in any lesson or assignment;
- a learning outcome is not tested in any **summative** assessment;
- a summative assessment tests a learning outcome in an earlier week than the first lesson about it;
- an assessment tests a learning outcome below that outcome's Bloom level;
- a lesson or assignment serves no learning outcome;
- the weights of the summative assessments do not add up to 100%;
- the planned hours exceed the study load (one EC is 28 hours of study).

The checks are **suggestions**. LimeOnIt changes nothing in your course; you decide. No AI is involved: the checks only work with what you fill in yourself.

## Bloom

The levels follow the revised Bloom taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001): remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate and create. A level is optional. Without one, LimeOnIt skips the level check for that learning outcome.

## The coverage matrix

On the course page is a table with the learning outcomes in the rows and the activities and assessments in the columns. A ✓ means the activity or assessment covers that learning outcome, with the level if you filled it in.

## Linking work

On a tool page with a learning-outcome field, pick your course under **Course**. LimeOnIt then fills in the course's learning outcomes, one per line; remove what doesn't fit. What you generate is linked to the course. You can also link or unlink work afterwards on **Earlier work**, to the whole course or to one activity or assessment. The course page lists it all under **Linked work**.

## Get a review

Under **Review** on the course page, two critical colleagues look at your course: an **Assessment expert** (assessment blueprint, Bloom spread, wording, weighting) and a **Designer** (constructive alignment, active learning, study load). They are AI and run on the model you pick.

- Each piece of advice points at a learning outcome, activity or assessment in your own course, or at the course as a whole.
- For each piece of advice you choose **Agree** or **Disagree**, with a reason if you like. Agreeing only records your decision; nothing in your course changes.
- Advice you disagreed with does not come back in a later review of the same course.
- If you change the course after a review, you see a notice. Then get a new review.

The fixed checks above stay facts; the review is an opinion you weigh against your own judgement.
