# Your review team: who reviews your course

Your review team works by the four-eyes principle: a second look at your course before you teach it. Each **reviewer** is a critical colleague who reviews your course from one field, such as assessment or course design. A reviewer is AI and runs on the model you pick. It gives advice; you decide what to do with each piece of advice. Nothing in your course changes.

You get a review on the course page, under **Review team**. How that works in your course is in [Courses](/help/courses).

## Choosing who is on your review team

Above **Get a review**, **Who is on your review team?** lists each reviewer with one line on what it looks at. Tick the reviewers you want.

- LimeOnIt remembers your choice for all your courses. You can change it at every review.
- A reviewer you switch off no longer reviews. Its earlier advice is kept and comes back when you switch it on again.
- If you tick no reviewer at all, you cannot get a review.
- Your school's admin can switch off a reviewer, or the whole review team, for everyone. You then don't see that reviewer in the list.

More reviewers means more advice, but a review also takes longer. Pick the reviewers that fit your course right now.

## Assessment expert

The Assessment expert reviewer looks at your course's assessment like an experienced assessment expert from an examination board. The question is: do your assessments measure what your learning outcomes promise?

- **Testable learning outcomes:** one observable action, with a verb you can test ("analyses", not "understands") that fits the Bloom level.
- **Assessment form:** an outcome at *evaluate* or *create* asks for a product, assignment or oral exam, not a multiple-choice test.
- **Formative before summative:** do students get practice and feedback before it counts?
- **Weighting and spread:** does the weight fit what an assessment covers, and does the grade not hang on one moment?
- **Tags from your subject pack**, such as a proficiency level: does the assessment fit them?
- **Linked test questions**, only when you tick *Also review the linked test questions*: do the questions test at the level the outcome promises?

This reviewer is on by default.

## Designer

The Designer reviewer looks at your course design like an experienced educational designer. The question is: do your lessons and assignments lead students to the learning outcomes?

- **Constructive alignment:** do students do in class what the outcome asks? Applying and designing need practice, not just listening.
- **Active learning:** is there more than instruction for each outcome?
- **Build-up and order:** does instruction come before the practice that relies on it, and is there time between practice and assessment?
- **Study load and spread:** are the hours doable (1 EC is 28 hours), and is there no peak in one week?
- **Coherence:** does an activity fit the learning outcome it is linked to?

This reviewer is on by default.

## AI resilience

The AI resilience reviewer looks at your summative assessments like an assessment expert who knows what AI can do. The question is: does the grade show what students can do themselves, now that a student can have AI write a text, code or report?

- **Supervised or unsupervised:** for each assessment you can fill in whether it is *supervised* (exam, oral, practical in class) or *unsupervised* (work made at home). This reviewer looks mainly at unsupervised summative work.
- **What the grade rests on:** does a large part of the grade hang on a submitted product alone, without a conversation or a supervised moment?
- **The process in view:** are there drafts, feedback moments or a log, so you can see how the work came about?
- **A short check:** sometimes a short conversation or oral explanation is enough to see that the work is the student's own.

This reviewer does not ban AI and does not recommend AI detectors. It is fine with unsupervised formative work: that is where a student can practise, AI included. Whether the assessment form fits the outcome it leaves to the Assessment expert; the build-up of your lessons to the Designer.

This reviewer is off by default. Tick it if your course has summative work made at home.

## Professional practice

The Professional practice reviewer looks at your course like a teacher with years of experience in the profession. The question is: do students practise and show what a starting professional does, at the level your subject pack asks?

- **A professional situation:** do students practise a professional activity from an outcome (such as analysing, advising or designing) on a case, client or practice question, and not just in a lecture?
- **An assessment that resembles the work:** for the grade, does the student make something a professional also makes, such as advice, a design or a product?
- **The proficiency level:** do the independence and complexity of assignment and assessment fit the level from your pack? A step-by-step assignment does not belong at level 3; at level 1 a guided assignment is right.

This reviewer leans on the tags from your subject pack for each outcome, such as the professional activities and the proficiency level. If you did not fill them in, it says so and only checks whether practice and assessment resemble the profession. How you word the assessment it leaves to the Assessment expert; build-up and study load to the Designer.

This reviewer is off by default. Tick it if your course prepares for a profession with a subject pack.

## What a reviewer sees

A reviewer sees only this one course, as you filled it in:

- the learning outcomes, with Bloom level and the tags from your subject pack;
- the lessons, assignments and assessments, with week, hours, weight and the outcomes they serve (if you filled in an assessment's questions, that follows from them);
- the result of the fixed checks, so the reviewer does not repeat them;
- advice you disagreed with earlier, so the reviewer does not give it again.

Linked work, such as test questions you made with a tool, only goes along when you tick that at the review. A reviewer never sees other courses, your chats or students' work.

## What a reviewer does not do

- **Change anything.** Agree only records your decision. What you take on, you change yourself.
- **Make things up.** A reviewer names no rules, norms or sources that are not in your course. If your course is in good shape, it gives little or no advice.
- **Repeat itself.** Advice you disagreed with does not come back for the same course.
- **Judge you.** A reviewer looks at the course, not at you as a teacher.

A reviewer gives at most eight pieces of advice, the most important first. *Note* means students will probably not reach the outcome this way or the assessment does not add up; *Tip* is an improvement.
