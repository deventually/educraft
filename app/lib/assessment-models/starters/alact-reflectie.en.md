Reflection cycle (ALACT) — starter model for feedforward on a reflection report. For feedback only, never for a grade. Adapt the criteria to your own programme's assignment.

Source: Korthagen's ALACT model, in Korthagen & Kessels (1999), "Linking theory and practice: Changing the pedagogy of teacher education", Educational Researcher 28(4), 4–17, https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X028004004. The five phases below follow that model; the descriptions are a LimeOnIt summary, not a quotation.

C1 — Action (phase 1).
The student describes a concrete situation from practice: what happened, where, and what the student did.

C2 — Looking back on the action (phase 2).
The student looks back on the situation: what the student thought, felt, wanted and did, and what the others in the situation may have thought, felt, wanted and done.

C3 — Awareness of essential aspects (phase 3).
The student names the core of the situation and what in it matters for their own actions.

C4 — Creating alternative methods of action (phase 4).
The student comes up with other ways of acting and chooses one, with a reason.

C5 — Trial (phase 5).
The student describes how and when the chosen alternative is tried out in a next situation; that starts a new cycle.
