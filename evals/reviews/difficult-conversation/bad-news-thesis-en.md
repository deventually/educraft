## Feedback on your conversation

### What went well
- "I hear that you're angry, and that you feel the feedback came late. Can you tell me more about that?" You named the emotion and invited me to say more, without defending yourself. I told you more afterwards, including the "looks good, keep going" comments.
- "Both examiners graded your thesis a 5.0, so it's a fail for now." After the warning in your opening, you gave the news plainly. As a student, I knew where I stood.
- "Before we wrap up: in your own words, what are the main things to change?" This let you check what I had understood instead of assuming it. It showed I had understood the three points but still had an open question.

### What could be better
- "Okay. Let me explain exactly how we got to this grade. ..." I was still angry and worried about my family. A long explanation followed by a to-do list made me feel like I was getting a list. You could say: "I'll explain how we got to the grade, but first: what you just said about your family matters. Shall I start with the main point?"
- "The resit is in eight weeks. What would help you most to get there?" I had just told you I felt like I'd failed everybody. You moved on to the plan straight away, so my embarrassment went unanswered. You could say: "That sounds heavy, carrying that towards your parents and your manager. Take a moment. Shall we look at the plan after that?"
- "Let's plan a meeting in two weeks where you bring a revised chapter 4. Does that work for you?" I asked twice who would check my work, and you never answered. I also said eight weeks felt tight. You could say: "Good question. I'll find out with the supervisor who will give feedback and come back to you on that. Is two weeks realistic given your job?"
