## Rewritten assignment

### What are you going to do?

You write a **policy brief** of 1500 words ±10%, excluding the reference list. You write it individually. This assignment builds on the theoretical perspectives on evidence-informed policymaking from weeks 1–4.

Your policy brief is addressed to a municipal policy maker of your choice. In it you do three things:

- You analyse a local policy problem.
- You critically compare at least two policy options, in light of the available evidence.
- You give a reasoned recommendation.

[to be filled in by the teacher: why this assignment? What does it add for the course or for the profession?]

### Steps

1. Choose a local policy problem and a municipal policy maker as the reader of your policy brief.
2. Write a draft of your policy brief.
3. In week 5, upload your draft. [to be filled in by the teacher: where do students upload the draft, and by which day and time?]
4. In week 5, write feedback on the drafts of two fellow students. [to be filled in by the teacher: how are the two peers assigned, where and in what form is the feedback given, and by which day and time?]
5. Finish your final version.
6. Submit your final version in Canvas as a .docx file. The deadline is Friday of week 7, 23:59.

### Requirements

**Product**
- You write the policy brief individually.
- The length is 1500 words ±10%. The reference list does not count.
- The policy brief is addressed to a municipal policy maker of your choice.
- You analyse a local policy problem.
- You critically compare at least two policy options, in light of the available evidence.
- You give a reasoned recommendation.

**Sources**
- You use a minimum of six peer-reviewed sources.
- You reference them according to APA 7th edition.

**Figure or table**
- You may include one figure or table. This is permitted but not required.

**Peer feedback (condition for passing)**
- In week 5 you take part in the peer feedback round. You upload a draft and you give written feedback on the drafts of two fellow students.
- Giving feedback to two peers is a condition for passing the assignment.

### Submission and assessment

**Submission**
- Where: Canvas
- File type: .docx
- Deadline: Friday of week 7, 23:59

**Assessment**

Your work is assessed with this rubric:

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Problem analysis | 30% |
| Use and appraisal of evidence | 30% |
| Quality of the recommendation | 20% |
| Fit to audience and style | 20% |

You pass the assignment with a weighted mark of 5.5 or higher. The weighted mark is your final mark after each criterion is counted by its percentage. You also need to meet the peer feedback condition (see Requirements).

## Open points

1. You name no purpose or value for this assignment. Students then do not know what it adds for the course or for the profession. The "Why this assignment?" section only holds a marker.
2. You name no deadline for the week 5 draft upload, and no day or time for the peer feedback. Students cannot plan the round without these.
3. You do not say where students upload the draft, how the two peers are assigned, or where and in what form the feedback is given. You also name no length, file type or criteria for the feedback. Students need this to meet the condition for passing.
4. The condition says "participation in this round, i.e. providing feedback to two peers". It is unclear whether uploading your own draft is also required to pass. I kept your wording. Students will ask this.
5. The criterion "critically compared" is not observable. It is unclear what counts as critical. Suggestion: describe what a critical comparison shows, for example which comparison points are used.
6. "Available evidence" and "reasoned recommendation" are not observable. Students do not know what evidence suffices or what a good reason looks like. Suggestion: state which kinds of evidence count and what the recommendation must refer to.
7. The rubric criteria have no descriptions or levels. "Quality of the recommendation" and "Fit to audience and style" are not observable. Students cannot tell what a pass or a high mark looks like. Suggestion: add a short description per criterion and per level.
8. You do not say whether the word count includes title, headings, in-text citations, or the caption of a figure or table. You only say the reference list is excluded. Students need this to stay within 1500 words ±10%.
9. You do not say whether the six peer-reviewed sources may include course literature. They also may be fewer than the sources used for the theory. Students need this to plan their search.
10. The mark scale is not stated, so 5.5 has no frame of reference for international students. You also say nothing about resits or late submission. Students need to know what happens if they fail or miss the deadline.
11. You name no contact person or place for questions. Students need to know whom to ask.
12. The meaning of "local policy problem" and "municipal policy maker of their choosing" is open. It is unclear, for example, whether a real person or a role is meant, and whether the problem must be in a Dutch municipality. Suggestion: state what is allowed.

## What I changed

- **Structure:** I put the text under fixed headings. The product, sources, optional figure or table and peer feedback each now have a visible place.
- **Steps numbered:** the original describes the sequence in running text. It now runs in order from choosing a problem to submitting.
- **Long sentences shortened:** the opening sentence is now a short intro plus a list of three actions.
- **Hidden requirement made visible:** the condition on peer feedback was in a subordinate clause ("i.e. … constitutes a condition"). It now has its own heading and is repeated under assessment.
- **Submission information together:** Canvas, .docx and deadline are in one place.
- **Wording simplified:** "students are required to produce" became "you write". "Draw upon" became "use". "Is permitted but not required" became "You may include … This is permitted but not required."
- **Term explained:** "weighted mark" is explained in one sentence. "Policy brief" and "peer-reviewed" stayed word for word.
- **Gaps marked:** I added markers for the purpose, the draft and feedback logistics, and the contact person. I invented no content.
