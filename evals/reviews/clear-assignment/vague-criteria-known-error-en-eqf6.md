## Rewritten assignment

### What are you going to do?

You write a literature review on your own. A literature review is a text that sums up and discusses what researchers have written about one topic.

You choose one topic from the list on the course page. Your review is 3000–3500 words long.

### Why this assignment?

[to be filled in by the teacher: why this assignment?]

### Steps

1. Choose one topic from the list on the course page.
2. Find your sources. Use at least 15 sources. At least 10 of them must be peer-reviewed journal articles. (Peer-reviewed means that other experts checked the article before it was published. A journal is a publication with scientific articles.)
3. Write your literature review on your own. It has 3000–3500 words.
4. Use Harvard referencing throughout. (Harvard referencing is a fixed way to show which sources you used.)
5. Submit your review via Moodle by 30 May, 17:00.

### Requirements

- You work individually.
- You write on one of the topics on the course page.
- Your review has 3000–3500 words.
- You use at least 15 sources.
- At least 10 of these sources are peer-reviewed journal articles.
- You use Harvard referencing throughout.

**Submission**

## Open points

1. You name no purpose for this assignment. Students do not see what it adds for the subject or the profession. This is why "Why this assignment?" holds only a marker.
2. The criterion "of good quality" is not observable. Students cannot tell what good quality looks like. Suggestion: describe what a good review contains, for example in a short rubric.
3. The criterion "sufficiently critical of the literature" is not observable. Students do not know how much criticism is enough or what it looks like. Suggestion: say what students must show, for example comparing sources or naming limits of studies.
4. The criterion "a professional attitude is shown" is not observable. Students cannot tell which behaviour or work counts. Suggestion: name the observable behaviours that count, or remove the criterion.
5. The criterion "clearly made an effort" is not observable. Effort is hard to see in a finished text. Suggestion: link it to something visible, such as use of sources or a draft handed in.
6. You name no weighting for the four criteria. Students do not know which criterion counts most for the grade.
7. You name no file type, such as Word or PDF. Students do not know in which form to submit.
8. You do not say where in Moodle students submit (for example, which assignment page). They may not find the right place.
9. You name no contact person or way to ask questions. Students do not know whom to ask when something is unclear.
10. You do not say whether the 3000–3500 words include the reference list, title page and appendices. This affects how long the text can be.
11. You do not say where on the course page the topic list is, or whether topics must be approved. Students may not know how to choose.
12. You say nothing about late submission, conditions for a resit or a resit. Students do not know what happens after a fail or a missed deadline. Add this only if it applies.
13. The deadline "30 May, 17:00" has no year. This matters only if the text is reused in later years.
14. The term "journal" is explained in everyday words by me. Check that this explanation fits what you mean by "peer-reviewed journal article".

## What I changed

- I started with what students do and added a short explanation of "literature review".
- I split long sentences into short ones with one action each.
- I put the work in numbered steps in the order students do it. I took the steps only from the original text.
- I explained "peer-reviewed" and "Harvard referencing" in one short sentence each, and kept both terms word for word. I also explained "journal".
- I put all requirements in one list. The condition "of which at least 10 must be peer-reviewed journal articles" was in a subordinate clause. It is now a separate item.
- I put all submission information and the assessment information together. The criteria stay a list, addressed to "you" (for example, "the student is sufficiently critical" became "you are sufficiently critical").
- I added markers for what is missing: the purpose, the file type, the place in Moodle and a contact person.
- I did not change the criteria. I only put suggestions under "Open points".
