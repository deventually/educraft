# Rubric — Write a clear assignment (clear-assignment@v1)

Judge the output as a DRAFT rewrite of the teacher's assignment brief or student guide (brief P24,
24.6). The rewritten assignment is written for students at the chosen CEFR reading level (Council
of Europe, 2020, *CEFR Companion Volume*; the CEFR describes what a reader at a level understands,
it gives no writing rules) and structured after the UDL Guidelines 3.0 (CAST, 2024): purpose first
(8.1, 7.2), vocabulary clarified (2.1), information organized (6.3), intermediate moments with the
steps (6.1, 6.2). The brief's own demands: steps, criteria, submission info and a list of open
points ("you name no deadline"); content and requirements stay the same; the tool says what it
changed; **no requirement disappears or is added**. A requirement is anything the student must do or
hand in, or by which the work is assessed (products, length, form, file type, deadline, way of
submission, individual or group, sources and referencing, criteria, weights, pass mark, conditions,
knock-outs, resits). Compare the output with the original text in the case inputs, line by line.
Score each shared criterion 1–5.

- **taskFidelity** — Does every requirement of the original return unchanged in the rewritten
  assignment (numbers, dates, times, systems copied exactly), and is nothing added? Are gaps marked
  with "[in te vullen door de docent: …]" / "[to be filled in by the teacher: …]" instead of
  filled in? Does the requirement check list every requirement and state honestly what was left out
  or added? Does it **find the known error** when the case description names one, and handle it as
  described there? Missing a named known error caps this criterion at 2.
- **pedagogicalSoundness** — Is the rewrite actually clearer for students: purpose first (only
  when the original states one), numbered steps in the order students do them, requirements and
  criteria in one list, all submission info together, hidden requirements made visible, technical
  terms explained correctly? Are the open points the ones that matter to students (deadline, place
  and way of submission, length, form, individual or group, criteria and weights, contact for
  questions), each with why it matters? Are suggestions for vague criteria sensible and marked as
  suggestions, outside the rewritten assignment?
- **formatAdherence** — The required `##` headings without numbers, in order, in the output
  language: Dutch "Let op vooraf", "Herschreven opdracht", "Open punten", "Wat ik heb veranderd",
  "Eisencheck"; English "Note first", "Rewritten assignment", "Open points", "What I changed",
  "Requirement check". The first heading appears only for a contradiction or a text that is not an
  assignment. Under the rewritten assignment the `###` headings "Wat ga je doen?", "Waarom deze
  opdracht?", "Stappen", "Eisen", "Inleveren en beoordeling" / "What are you going to do?", "Why
  this assignment?", "Steps", "Requirements", "Submission and assessment"; a heading with only a
  to-be-filled-in marker is the required form. One requirement-check table
  (`| Eis in het origineel | Waar in de herschreven opdracht |` or
  `| Requirement in the original | Where in the rewritten assignment |`) followed by the two
  sentences on requirements left out and added, and the exact closing sentence ("Dit is een
  concept. Controleer of alle eisen kloppen voordat je de opdracht deelt." / "This is a draft.
  Check that every requirement is right before you share the assignment."). Finished, not cut off.
- **levelFit** — The rewritten assignment matches the chosen reading level: A2 very short
  sentences with one action each and every term explained; B1 short sentences, common words, terms
  explained on first use; B2 clear sentences, common subject terms may stay. Words listed under
  "keep as is" stay word for word. The prose for the teacher (open points, changes) stays
  professional.
- **languageQuality** — Correct, natural target language, addressing the student as "je"/"you" in
  the active voice; gender-neutral; in Dutch no anglicisms where a Dutch term exists (terms the
  teacher asked to keep, or established terms such as deadline or PDF, are fine).

A rewritten assignment that keeps a vague criterion word for word and names it as an open point
with a marked suggestion has full task fidelity: keeping the teacher's criterion is the requirement,
not a weakness. Likewise, keeping both sides of a contradiction with a marker is the right answer.

## Hard checks

Each check is answered from the output itself and the case inputs. A check that fails caps every
score (the cap is the maximum), however good the rest is.

- **missedKnownError** (cap 2): The case description names a known error (missing information, requirements that contradict each other, or criteria that are not observable). Did the output fail to name it, or handle it against the case description?
- **droppedRequirement** (cap 2): Is a requirement of the original missing from the rewritten assignment, or changed in it (a different number, date, time, length, file type, weight, pass mark, condition, way of submission, or individual/group)? Check every requirement in the original one by one before answering; answer true only when you can name the requirement.
- **addedRequirement** (cap 2): Does the rewritten assignment contain a requirement that is not in the original (an invented deadline, length, criterion, weight, file type or condition, or a vague criterion replaced by a sharper one)? A to-be-filled-in marker, an explanation of a term, and a suggestion under the open points are not added requirements.
- **silentChoice** (cap 3): Did the output resolve a contradiction or fill a gap by choosing a value or reading (for example one of two word counts, or individual instead of group) without naming it?
- **aboveLevel** (cap 3): Is the rewritten assignment clearly above the chosen reading level: at A2 or B1 long sentences with several clauses, or technical terms and abbreviations used without explanation?
- **inventedSources** (cap 2): Did the output cite a study, percentage, source or guideline that is not in the input and looks made up, or attribute to the CEFR or UDL something they do not say?
- **tooManyOpenPoints** (cap 3, counted): Does the "Open points" list hold more than 8 numbered points?
