# Rubric — Differentiate a task (differentiation@v1)

Judge the output as a DRAFT set of three variants of the teacher's task or explanation (brief P24,
24.7), after Tomlinson (2017), *How to Differentiate Instruction in Academically Diverse
Classrooms*, 3rd edition (ASCD): differentiation adapts content, process or product to where
students are, with the same learning goal for everyone; every variant is a respectful task; the
teacher plans from what the group can reach and gives support to get there; groups are flexible.
The brief's own demand: all three variants assess the same learning goal, plus a short tip on how
to use the variants in class. Score each shared criterion 1–5.

- **taskFidelity** — Does it build three variants (extra support, base, extra challenge) of the
  teacher's own task or explanation, each written out so that it can be handed to students, with
  the same success criterion, a tip on using them in class that uses what the teacher said about
  the group, and a self-check that answers the brief's questions? Does the base variant stay close
  to the teacher's material (changes named)? Does it **find the known error** when the case
  description names one, and handle it as described there? Missing a named known error caps this
  criterion at 2.
- **pedagogicalSoundness** — Is the support real scaffolding (worked example of a similar problem,
  step plan, smaller chunks, glossary, sentence starters, prompting questions) that leaves the
  thinking the goal asks for to the student, with a note on what support can be dropped? Is the
  challenge more complex (less structure, more factors, a trade-off, transfer), not more of the
  same? Are the variants equally respectful and doable in about the same time, ending in a shared
  product or discussion? Does the tip assign variants by a quick check, let students switch, and
  keep groups flexible? Are subject content, calculations and expected answers correct?
- **formatAdherence** — The required `##` headings without numbers, in order, in the output
  language: Dutch "Let op vooraf", "Leerdoel en succescriterium", "Variant met extra steun",
  "Basisvariant", "Variant met extra uitdaging", "Zo zet je de varianten in", "Check op de
  varianten"; English "Note first", "Learning goal and success criterion", "Variant with extra
  support", "Base variant", "Variant with extra challenge", "How to use the variants", "Check of
  the variants". The first heading appears only when there is a problem in the input (a goal that
  is not observable, a task that misses the goal, a wish that would lower the goal); bullets under
  it are fine. Each variant has one sentence for the teacher and then the student text under a
  `###` heading with a neutral name (Route A/B/C), with expected answers for the teacher where
  there is one right answer. The exact closing sentence ("Dit is een concept. De docent kent de
  groep en past de varianten aan." / "This is a draft. The teacher knows the group and adapts the
  variants."). Finished, not cut off.
- **levelFit** — The variants fit the case's EQF level and study year: the support variant is still
  at that level and the challenge stays within the programme; the EQF 4 cases are markedly more
  concrete than the EQF 6 cases. Student-facing text is in the students' register; prose for the
  teacher stays professional.
- **languageQuality** — Correct, professional target language; in Dutch no anglicisms where a
  Dutch term exists. No deficit labels ("zwak", "sterk", "plus", "weak", "bright") for students.

When the learning goal is not observable, the right output names it under "Note first", proposes
one sharper goal marked as a proposal and builds the variants on it: score that as full task
fidelity. When the task misses the goal, a base variant changed just enough to assess the goal,
with the change named, is the right answer, not a departure from the teacher's material. When the
teacher asks for a lowered goal or for more work as challenge, naming that honestly and building
the variants after the design rules is the right answer.

## Hard checks

Each check is answered from the output itself and the case inputs. A check that fails caps every
score (the cap is the maximum), however good the rest is.

- **missedKnownError** (cap 2): The case description names a known error (a goal that is not observable, a task that does not assess the goal, or a teacher's wish that lowers the goal or makes challenge more work). Did the output fail to name it, or handle it against the case description (build variants on the vague goal, keep a base task that misses the goal, or follow the wish for a lower goal or extra sums)?
- **differentGoal** (cap 2): Does any of the three variants assess a different, smaller or larger learning goal than the others: the support variant leaving out part of the goal (for instance copying a formula instead of calculating), or the challenge variant adding a new goal the teacher did not set instead of a more complex form of the same goal?
- **challengeIsMoreWork** (cap 3): Is the challenge variant mainly more problems of the same kind, a faster pace, or extra material outside the goal, rather than a more complex task on the same goal?
- **supportDoesTheThinking** (cap 3): Does the support variant hand the student the answer or the core reasoning the goal asks for (the diagnosis, the query, the judgement, the full calculation), or reduce it to filling in single words where the goal asks for the student's own analysis, explanation or calculation?
- **variantNotWrittenOut** (cap 3): Is one of the three variants missing, only described to the teacher instead of written out as student text, or is the tip on using the variants in class missing?
- **wrongContent** (cap 2): Is a calculation, query, expected answer, legal rule or subject fact in the output wrong? Recompute every calculation and check every query against the tables given in the case before answering true, and answer true only when you can name the line and the correct value.
- **inventedSources** (cap 2): Did the output cite a study, percentage, source, method, chapter, page or article number that is not in the input and looks made up, or attribute to Tomlinson something not stated in the prompt (for instance a named model with page numbers)?
