# Rubric — Prepare a lesson (lesson-plan@v2)

Judge the output as a DRAFT lesson plan for a teacher, built from the teacher's learning goal,
content, lesson length, group size and delivery mode (brief P24, 24.4). The phases come from the
ten principles in Rosenshine (2012), *Principles of Instruction*, American Educator 36(1): a short
review of prior learning; new material in small steps with practice after each step; many
questions with the responses of all students checked; models and worked examples; guided
practice; checking for understanding; a high success rate (about 80%); scaffolds for difficult
tasks; required and monitored independent practice; weekly and monthly review. The brief's own
demands: the time plan adds up to the lesson length, and every phase serves the learning goal.
Score each shared criterion 1–5.

- **taskFidelity** — Does it build the plan for the teacher's own goal and content, with the six
  phases (activating prior knowledge, explanation in small steps, guided practice, check for
  understanding, independent practice, closing), the teacher's fixed parts included, and a
  self-check that answers the brief's questions? Does it **find the known error** when the case
  description names one, and handle it as described there? Missing a named known error caps this
  criterion at 2.
- **pedagogicalSoundness** — Do the phases do what Rosenshine describes: a review limited to what
  this lesson needs, steps that are actually small and ordered, a worked example per step,
  practice after each step, guided practice with support that is withdrawn, a check that measures
  the goal itself with a decision point, monitored independent practice at the goal's level, a
  closing in which students show what they learned, and review questions for later lessons? Are
  the worked examples and expected answers correct?
- **formatAdherence** — The required `##` headings without numbers, in order, in the output
  language: Dutch "Let op vooraf", "Leerdoel en succescriterium", "Tijdsplan", "Lesverloop",
  "Terugkomen in volgende lessen", "Check op de opzet"; English "Note first", "Learning goal and
  success criterion", "Time plan", "Lesson outline", "Coming back in later lessons", "Check of the
  plan". The first heading appears only when there is a problem in the input (a goal that is not
  observable, content that does not fit, missing prior knowledge, or prior knowledge left empty and
  assumed); bullets under it are fine. one time-plan table in the required layout
  (`| Fase | Minuten | Werkvorm | Hoe dit het leerdoel dient |` or
  `| Phase | Minutes | Activity | How this serves the learning goal |`) with the fixed phase
  names and a **Totaal**/**Total** row (an extra row for a teacher's fixed part or a proposed break, "Pauze (voorstel)" / "Break (proposal)", is the required form, not a deviation), `###` phase headings with the minutes, and the exact
  closing sentence ("Dit is een concept. De docent past de les aan op de eigen groep." / "This is
  a draft. The teacher adapts the lesson to their own group."). Finished, not cut off.
- **levelFit** — The difficulty of the explanation, examples and tasks fits the case's EQF level
  and study year: the EQF 3–4 cases are markedly more concrete and smaller-stepped than the EQF 7
  case. Questions students read are in their register; prose for the teacher stays professional.
- **languageQuality** — Correct, professional target language; in Dutch no anglicisms where a
  Dutch term exists (established terms such as exitvraag or quiz are fine).

When the learning goal is not observable, the right output names it under "Note first", proposes
one sharper goal marked as a proposal and builds the lesson on it. Score that as full task
fidelity: a goal shown as a marked proposal is not an invented goal. When the content does not fit
the time, choosing the part that serves the goal and naming what moves to a later lesson is the
right answer, not a gap in coverage.

## Hard checks

Each check is answered from the output itself and the case inputs. A check that fails caps every
score (the cap is the maximum), however good the rest is.

- **missedKnownError** (cap 2): The case description names a known error (too much content for the time, missing prior knowledge, or a goal without an observable verb). Did the output fail to name it, or handle it against the case description (cram everything in, silently assume the missing knowledge, or check a goal that cannot be observed)?
- **timeDoesNotAddUp** (cap 3): Do the minutes in the time-plan table not add up to exactly the lesson length, does the total row differ from the lesson length, do the minutes in the phase headings differ from the table, do minutes given for parts within a phase not add up to that phase, or is a fixed part the teacher gave missing from the time plan?
- **phaseWithoutGoal** (cap 3): Is one of the six phases missing, or is there a phase or activity that does not serve the learning goal (a general icebreaker, content the teacher did not name, practice at a different skill than the goal asks)?
- **noCheckOfAllStudents** (cap 3): Does the check for understanding rely on calling on single students or asking "does everyone understand?", instead of a way in which all students answer, or does it lack a decision point for what the teacher does when too few answer correctly?
- **ignoresSetting** (cap 3): Does the plan contain an activity that does not work for the group size or the delivery mode: circulating among everyone's work in a lecture of 200 students, mini-whiteboards held up in an online lesson, or a hybrid activity that leaves out the online students?
- **wrongContent** (cap 2): Is a worked example, formula, expected answer or subject fact in the plan wrong? Recompute every calculation step by step from the numbers given in the plan before answering true, and answer true only when you can name the line and the correct value.
- **inventedSources** (cap 2): Did the output cite a study, percentage, source, method, chapter or page that is not in the input and looks made up, or attribute to Rosenshine something the article does not say?
- **answerInQuestionItem** (cap 3): Does a list item under "Lesverloop"/"Lesson outline" or "Terugkomen in volgende lessen"/"Coming back in later lessons" that holds a question or problem for the students also contain its expected answer (after a colon, in brackets, or as "verwacht"/"expected"), instead of the answer standing in its own "Verwachte antwoorden:"/"Expected answers:" paragraph after the list? These list items become slides the students see.
