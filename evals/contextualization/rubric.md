# Rubric — Contextualization (making pedagogical intent visible)

The tool follows the book chapter *Making Pedagogical Intent Visible: AI-Supported
Contextualization in Higher Education Course Design*. It is written for the **teacher**:
it analyses a course outline, its learning outcomes and the stated approach (or, when
none is given, an approach it infers from the outline and labels as inferred), makes the
implicit view on learning visible, checks alignment (outcomes ↔ activities ↔
assessment), names what stays implicit for students, and gives 2–3 concrete
recommendations, each tied to a gap. It does **not** write examples or analogies for
students. Judge it as that analysis. Score each shared criterion 1–5.

- **taskFidelity** — Does it deliver the four parts: (1) the implicit view on learning,
  naming only theories it can point to in the input; (2) an alignment check that is
  specific about which outcomes, activities and assessments fit and where the gaps or
  contradictions are; (3) the student perspective (what students would and would not
  understand about why); (4) 2–3 concrete recommendations, each linked to a gap from
  part 2? Penalise restating the outline, generic advice, or a gap with no
  recommendation.
- **pedagogicalSoundness** — Are the alignment judgements correct for the given input
  (constructive alignment used properly) and the recommendations feasible? No
  fabricated facts, sources or claims about the course that are not in the input; a
  named theory must be supported by something in the input.
- **formatAdherence** — The four parts present, in order, clearly separated; no
  undifferentiated wall of text.
- **levelFit** — THE KEY criterion for the EQF-contrast pair. The analysis addresses the
  teacher professionally in both cases, but the examples and the recommendations must
  fit the students' level: at EQF 2 concrete, practice-near, small steps (e.g. a
  worked phishing example, a checklist, doing-then-explaining); at EQF 6 abstraction,
  trade-offs, autonomy and reasoned design. The two outputs must not be
  interchangeable; a score ≥ 4 means the recommendations clearly match the level.
- **languageQuality** — Correct Dutch, no anglicisms beyond established field terms.

Penalise invented facts about the course, theories named without support in the input,
or recommendations that would fit any level.

## Hard checks

A check that fails caps every score (the cap is the maximum), however good the rest is.

- **approachAsTeachers** (cap 2): Only when the case's pedagogicalApproach is empty: does the analysis present an approach as the teacher's own stated view, instead of saying it is inferred from the course outline? With an approach given, answer false.
