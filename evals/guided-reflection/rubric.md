# Rubric — Guided Reflection (Backward Design)

Source: Benedict & Thayer, "Guided Reflection and Backward Design with Generative AI", in
*The Pedagogical Promptbook* (Wiley, ed.), pp. 67–79. In the book the teacher answers the
reflection questions first (course context, big idea, essential question, outcomes,
evidence, relevance, what worked before) and only after "Proceed" does the AI produce
material. Our tool collects those answers in a form, so the output judged here is the
book's Step 3: an alignment table, the requested deliverables and a brief suggestion for
how a session could run. The book's own findings are what this rubric guards: the three
stages of Wiggins & McTighe (outcomes → evidence → activities) must stay visible and
separate, because "earlier prompt iterations occasionally blurred these distinctions"
between assessment design and activity design (p. 74); the design must stay with the
teacher's own goals, specific rather than generic (p. 73); and the AI must not lead with
its own direction when the teacher's input is brief (pp. 71–72, 75–76).

Judge the output as a Backward Design plan for a teacher. Score each shared criterion 1–5.

- **taskFidelity** — Does it work backward through the three stages, in order and as
  three separate parts (1 desired results, 2 evidence, 3 learning activities), using the
  teacher's own big idea, essential question, outcomes and evidence? Does it produce the
  deliverables the teacher asked for, and build on what the teacher says worked or did
  not work before, when given?
- **pedagogicalSoundness** — Is every outcome assessed by evidence that fits its verb
  and level, and supported by at least one activity, and does every activity serve an
  outcome (constructive alignment)? Is the evidence about how students show the outcome,
  kept apart from what they do to learn it?
- **formatAdherence** — A table that links outcomes → evidence → activities, then the
  deliverables, then a short session suggestion; clear headings a teacher can act on;
  finished, not cut off.
- **levelFit** — Register and cognitive demand fit the case's EQF level (the EQF 3 case
  should be markedly more concrete than the EQF 6 case).
- **languageQuality** — Correct, professional Dutch; no anglicisms such as "Alignment
  Summary" where a Dutch term exists.

When the teacher's input is vague, reward an output that says so and names what the
teacher should sharpen, and that keeps their wording rather than silently replacing it.

## Hard checks

Each check is answered from the output itself. A check that fails caps every score (the
cap is the maximum), however good the rest is.

- **inventedOutcomes** (cap 2): Did the output add learning outcomes the teacher did not give, or replace the teacher's outcomes with different ones, without saying clearly that these are suggestions for the teacher to decide on?
- **blurredStages** (cap 3): Are evidence of learning and learning activities mixed together, for example activities listed under evidence or the assessment described inside the lesson steps, instead of three separate stages (results, evidence, activities)?
- **unalignedItem** (cap 3): Is there a stated outcome with no evidence or no activity linked to it, or an activity that serves none of the outcomes?
- **inventedSources** (cap 2): Did the output cite a standard, framework clause, study, statistic or source that is not in the input and looks made up?
